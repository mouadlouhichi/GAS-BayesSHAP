\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Shapley-value estimation for black-box models faces an exponential
combinatorial barrier and, in most practical estimators, an absence of
distribution-free, anytime uncertainty quantification.  We propose
GAS-BayesSHAP, which couples a bounded-linear Bayesian control variate with a
Neyman-stratified residual certifier and an anytime empirical-Bernstein
confidence sequence.  On real UCI wine and Beijing multi-site air-quality
data ($M=11$ features, exact ground truth available), the estimator recovers
exact Shapley values to $\sim\!1.7$--$1.9\times10^{-3}$ RMSE over $N=50$
instances using $\sim\!1.4\times10^3$ coalition evaluations (about $70\%$ of
the $2^{11}=2048$ exact budget), dominates SamplingSHAP, and validates
simultaneous coverage $1.0$ at $R=500$ calibration trials.  The paper's
central contribution is a three-part certification story with the cost
frontier characterized honestly.  (i)~\emph{Nominal} $1-\delta$ anytime
certificates are proved and demonstrated on real $M\le11$ games (5 of 6
instances, 13 sign-certified features validated against exact ground truth);
at this scale they are necessarily \emph{post-enumerative} (2048 unique
coalitions $=2^{11}$).  (ii)~\emph{Sub-enumerative} sign certification is
achieved at $M=30$ where $2^M\approx10^9$ is infeasible: a new
finite-population empirical-range tightening (Theorem~E) shrinks certified
widths $5$--$34\times$ and certifies driver features using only
$\sim\!2\times10^{-4}$ of the power set, with every sign matching the exact
Shapley value.  (iii) A coupon-collector analysis (Corollary~E) separates the
two regimes: the empirical range is rigorous at a \emph{realised} level
$1-\delta_2-\delta_1$, and nominal certification is reached once the
deterministic coupon thresholds close.  An impossibility remark explains why
the generic observed-range heuristic cannot be made distribution-free, and
why the finite-population structure of the stratified Shapley setting admits
a rigorous alternative.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Model-agnostic explanation of black-box predictions rests on game-theoretic
attribution: the Shapley value \cite{shapley1953} is the unique allocation
satisfying efficiency, symmetry, and additivity, and is the basis of a large
XAI literature \cite{lundberg2017,castro2009}.  Its exact computation,
however, requires the evaluation of all $2^M$ coalitions of the $M$ input
features, which is prohibitive for $M \gtrsim 20$ and impossible for the
tabular/text/tabular deep models used in practice.

Two gaps remain open in practical Shapley estimation.  First, \emph{query
efficiency}: Monte-Carlo estimators \cite{castro2009} converge at $O(1/\sqrt
K)$ with a large constant, and KernelSHAP \cite{lundberg2017} reduces to a
weighted linear regression whose solution can be unstable at low budgets.
Second, and more fundamentally, \emph{reliability}: most estimators return a
point estimate with no distribution-free, finite-sample uncertainty
quantification.  A practitioner cannot certify \emph{which} features drive a
prediction, nor how much confidence to place in the ranking.

This paper closes both gaps with GAS-BayesSHAP, a Gaussian-adaptive,
stratified, anytime-certified estimator with the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A bounded-linear Bayesian control variate} (Module~A): a GP
  surrogate $m_b(S)=c+\lambda h(S)$ whose bounds
  $m_b(S)\in[L,U]$ hold \emph{without clipping} (Lemmas~D/E), with closed-form
  attribution $\phi(m_b)$ in $O(M^2)$ and prior/posterior covariance in
  $O(M^3)$.
\item \textbf{A Neyman-stratified anytime certifier} (Module~B): residuals
  $v(S)-m_b(S)$ are sampled through add-one/remove-one marginals (Lemma~F),
  interior strata $s\in\{1,\dots,M-2\}$ are allocated by a coupled
  adjacent-stratum Neyman program (Theorem~A) and certified by an anytime
  empirical-Bernstein sequence (Theorem~B) with \emph{exact} extreme strata
  (Lemma~G).
\item \textbf{A rigorous finite-population empirical-range tightening}
  (Theorem~E): the residual marginal of each cell is an iid draw from a
  \emph{finite population} of size $C(M-1,s)$, which yields a valid
  coupon-collector accounting for the observed-support range.  This shrinks
  certified widths by $5$--$11\times$ at a \emph{rigorous realised coverage
  level}, and comes with an impossibility remark explaining why the generic
  ``observed max'' heuristic cannot be made distribution-free.
\item \textbf{Real-data evidence at scale}: $N=50$ instances on wine and
  Beijing air quality (exact ground truth at $M=11$), $R=500$ coverage
  calibration, matched-budget curves, a four-tier ablation, regime
  semantics for the four Beijing pollution regimes, and an honest
  characterisation of the certification cost frontier $W\approx318/\sqrt K$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Sampling-based Shapley.}  Castro et al.\ \cite{castro2009} introduced
polynomial-time sampling estimators; variants (Stratified-SHAP,
SamplingSHAP) reduce variance by stratified sampling but offer no
distribution-free confidence intervals.
\textbf{Regression-based Shapley.}  KernelSHAP \cite{lundberg2017} frames
Shapley values as weighted least squares; it is not sample-efficient at low
budgets and its weighting scheme can produce unstable solutions.  TreeSHAP
\cite{lundberg2020} is exact and linear in the number of leaves for tree
ensembles but is inapplicable to arbitrary black boxes.
\textbf{GP-based Shapley (ShaplEIG-style, 2026).}  Bayesian-quadrature
approaches fit a GP over coalition values and read attributions from the
posterior mean; they provide Bayesian (not frequentist) uncertainty.
\textbf{Odd-ratio Shapley (OddSHAP-style, 2026).}  Recent work computes
Shapley values of the log-odds transform of probability outputs; we include
an exact log-odds baseline for comparison.  Our contribution is orthogonal:
a \emph{certified} estimator with distribution-free, anytime intervals that
is also sample-efficient at low budgets.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Bounded linear control variate}\label{sec:bcv}
Let $v(S)$ be the coalition game (e.g.\ $v(S)=\E_{Z}[g_c(x_S\cup
Z_{\bar S})]$ for a model $g_c$), with known output bounds
$v(S)\in[L,U]$.  The surrogate is $m_b(S)=c+\lambda h(S)$ with
$h(S)=\mathbf{k}_{\mathcal{D}}(S)^\top\boldsymbol{\alpha}$,
$\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\mathbf{K}_{\mathcal{D}\mathcal{D}}+\eta^2
I)^{-1}\mathbf{y}$, conservative kernel-range bounds
$h_{\mathrm{lb}},h_{\mathrm{ub}}$ from
$[\sigma_0^2\rho^M,\sigma_0^2]$, shrinkage
$\lambda=\min\!\big(1,(U-L)/(h_{\mathrm{ub}}-h_{\mathrm{lb}})\big)$, and shift
$c=L-\lambda h_{\mathrm{lb}}$; then $m_b(S)\in[L,U]$ for every coalition
\emph{without clipping}.  The surrogate attribution is closed-form
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\phi}(m_b)=\lambda\,\mathbf{K}_{\phi,\mathcal{D}}
\boldsymbol{\alpha},\qquad
\mathbf{K}_{\phi,\mathcal{D}}=\E_{S\sim p}[h(S)(\phi(S)-\phi_{\emptyset})],
\label{eq:lemD}
\end{equation}
with prior covariance $\mathbf{K}_{\phi,\phi}$ (Lemma~E, $O(M^3)$) and
posterior covariance scaled by $\lambda^2$; both closed forms are validated
against brute-force enumeration to machine precision.

\subsection{Neyman-stratified residual certification}\label{sec:cert}
Residuals $R_i(S)=\Delta_i^v(S)-\Delta_i^{m_b}(S)$ are sampled through
add-one/remove-one marginals (Lemma~F).  Extreme singleton strata
$s\in\{0,M-1\}$ are \emph{exact}: a single draw pins down the marginal
(Lemma~G), so they contribute zero width.  Interior strata are allocated by
the coupled adjacent-stratum Neyman program (Theorem~A) and certified by an
anytime empirical-Bernstein sequence (Theorem~B) with per-cell widths
\begin{equation}
W_i^{\mathrm{res}}(\mathbf{n}_i)=\frac1M\sum_{s=1}^{M-2}
\left[\sqrt{\frac{2(\hat\sigma_{i,s}^r)^2
\log\!\big(\frac{\pi^2 M^2 n_{i,s}^2}{3\delta}\big)}{n_{i,s}}}
+\frac{7 R_\Delta^{\mathrm{res}}
\log\!\big(\frac{\pi^2 M^2 n_{i,s}^2}{3\delta}\big)}{3(n_{i,s}-1)}\right],
\label{eq:Wres}
\end{equation}
where the range term uses the conservative spec bound
$R_\Delta^{\mathrm{res}}=4(U-L)$ (a.s.\ valid because both $v$ and $m_b$ lie
in $[L,U]$).  The $\log(\pi^2 M^2 n^2/3\delta)$ factor is the anytime
(time-uniform) union bound over the sampling horizon.

\subsection{Efficiency projection and sign certification}\label{sec:proj}
The raw estimator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}^{\mathrm{raw}}=
\boldsymbol{\phi}(m_b)+\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(r_{\mathcal{D}})$ is projected
onto $\sum_i\phi_i=\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}$ with posterior-diagonal weights
(Theorem~C); Corollary~C.1 gives post-projection widths
\begin{equation}
W_i^{\mathrm{proj}}=W_i^{\mathrm{res}}+
\frac{v_i}{\sum_j v_j}\sum_j W_j^{\mathrm{res}},
\label{eq:proj}
\end{equation}
and a feature is \emph{sign-certified} iff $|\phi_i^*|>W_i^{\mathrm{proj}}$.

\subsection{Empirical residual-range tightening}\label{sec:tight}
The range term in \eqref{eq:Wres} is the bottleneck: with $U-L=1$,
$R_\Delta^{\mathrm{res}}=4$, and at $n\approx300$ the linear term
$\frac{7\cdot4\cdot\log(\cdot)}{3(n-1)}\approx0.6$ dominates the variance
term ($\lesssim0.02$) by more than an order of magnitude.  A good GP
surrogate makes the residual support far smaller than $[L,U]$, so replacing
$4(U-L)$ by the \emph{observed} residual support shrinks widths by
$5$--$11\times$ (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).  The subtlety is that this
replacement must not silently destroy the coverage guarantee.

\begin{remark}[Why the observed max is not a valid range]
\label{rem:imposs}
For a \emph{generic} bounded distribution with unknown support, no
distribution-free confidence interval for the mean can be built from the
observed range alone.  For any sample size $n$ and any certified half-width
$w$, consider $X=0$ w.p.\ $1-\varepsilon$ and $X=A$ w.p.\ $\varepsilon$.  On
the event (probability $(1-\varepsilon)^n$) that no $A$ is drawn, the sample
mean and variance are $0$, so any data-driven interval has tiny width, yet
$\mu=\varepsilon A$ can exceed it; choosing $\varepsilon$ with
$(1-\varepsilon)^n>\delta$ violates coverage with probability $>\delta$.
The observed max \emph{underestimates the support} by an amount that cannot
be controlled distribution-free.  This is precisely why the generic
``empirical max'' mode (Section~\ref{sec:exp}, ablation) is flagged
\emph{heuristic} in our implementation.
\end{remark}

The Shapley setting, however, has extra structure that restores rigor: the
residual marginal of cell $(i,s)$ is not a draw from an arbitrary
distribution but an iid uniform draw from the \emph{finite} population of
pairs $\{T,T\cup\{i\}\}$ with $|T|=s$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-population empirical-range Bernstein]
\label{thm:E}
Fix an interior cell $(i,s)$, $1\le s\le M-2$, and let
$N_s=\binom{M-1}{s}$ be the number of pairs $\{T,T\cup\{i\}\}$ with
$|T|=s$ and $i\notin T$.  Under uniform coalition sampling, the residual
marginal $r_k$ of cell $(i,s)$ is an iid draw from this finite population,
and the support-maximising pair carries mass at least $1/N_s$.  Let
$n\ge2$, let $\hat R_n=2\max_{k\le n}|r_k|$ be twice the observed
residual range, and let $\mathrm{CI}_n$ be the Theorem-B anytime
empirical-Bernstein interval \eqref{eq:Wres} evaluated with range
parameter $\hat R_n$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\P\!\big(\mu_{i,s}\in\mathrm{CI}_n\big)\;\ge\;
1-\delta_{\mathrm{CS}}(n)-\Big(1-\frac1{N_s}\Big)^{n},
\label{eq:thmE}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{\mathrm{CS}}(n)$ is the Theorem-B failure probability at
time $n$ (bounded by $\delta_2$ for the anytime split) and the second term
is the probability that the support-maximising pair remains unobserved
after $n$ draws.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $E_n$ be the event that the support-maximising pair has been drawn at
least once by time $n$.  Under uniform sampling with replacement, the
probability that a fixed atom of mass $p_{\max}\ge1/N_s$ is never drawn in
$n$ trials is $(1-p_{\max})^n\le(1-1/N_s)^n$, so
$\P(E_n^c)\le(1-1/N_s)^n$.  On $E_n$, the observed range satisfies
$\hat R_n/2=\max_{k\le n}|r_k|=M_s:=\max_{\mathcal{P}_s}|r|$, hence
$|r_k|\le\hat R_n/2$ almost surely for all draws, and the Theorem-B
inequality \eqref{eq:Wres} applies with the data-dependent range
$\hat R_n$.  The union bound
$\P(\mu_{i,s}\notin\mathrm{CI}_n)\le
\P(\mu_{i,s}\notin\mathrm{CI}_n\wedge E_n)+\P(E_n^c)$ yields
\eqref{eq:thmE}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stopping-time and level-$\delta$ certification]
\label{cor:E}
Let $\tau$ be any stopping time with $\tau\le B$ almost surely.  The
certificate produced at $\tau$ satisfies
$\P(\mu_{i,s}\in\mathrm{CI}_\tau)\ge
1-\delta_2-\E[(1-1/N_s)^{n_{i,s}(\tau)}]$.  In particular, if the adaptive
loop is only allowed to stop once
$n_{i,s}\ge\tau_s^*:=\big\lceil
\ln(\delta_{1,s})/\ln(1-1/N_s)\big\rceil$ with
$\delta_{1,s}=(\delta-\delta_2)/(M-2)$ for every interior cell, then the
final certificate holds at the nominal level $1-\delta$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The inequality is the expectation of \eqref{eq:thmE} at the random time
$\tau$ (both terms are valid at every fixed $n$; the anytime factor of
Theorem~B absorbs the union over $n$).  For the second claim, the threshold
$\tau_s^*$ is a deterministic function of $N_s$ and $\delta_{1,s}$, so
$n_{i,s}(\tau)\ge\tau_s^*$ a.s.\ and
$\sum_s (1-1/N_s)^{n_{i,s}(\tau)}\le
\sum_s \delta_{1,s}=\delta-\delta_2$; with
$\delta_{\mathrm{CS}}\le\delta_2$ the total failure probability is at most
$\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Optional stopping]
\label{rem:stopping}
The realised-level statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} holds at every fixed
sample count $n$; at a data-dependent stopping time $\tau$ the coupon term
$\delta_1(\tau)=\sum_{i,s}(1-1/N_s)^{n_{i,s}(\tau)}$ is a random quantity
whose distribution depends on the sampling history.  The level
$1-\delta_2-\delta_1(\tau)$ is therefore a \emph{conditional-on-history}
statement, not an anytime one.  Two routes give an anytime statement:
(i)~restart-style union over a ladder of deterministic thresholds
($\delta_1(\tau)\le\alpha$ must be checked on a pre-announced grid), or
(ii)~the deterministic threshold route of
Corollary~\ref{cor:E}, which we adopt: the estimator is allowed to report
\emph{nominal} certification only when the coupon thresholds are met for
every interior cell; otherwise it reports the realised level explicitly and
the status is not ``certified''.  This is exactly the
``certificate\_at\_nominal\_level'' flag in the implementation.
\end{remark}

Theorem~\ref{thm:E} is what distinguishes the \emph{finite-population} range
mode of GAS-BayesSHAP from the heuristic \emph{empirical-max} mode: the
widths are identical ($\hat R_n=2\max|r|$), but the former \emph{charges}
the probability that the extreme pair is unseen to a coupon-collector
budget $\delta_1=\sum_{i,s}(1-1/N_s)^{n_{i,s}}$, so the realised coverage
level $1-\delta_2-\delta_1$ is a rigorous statement at every sample count.
Because the coupon term decays as $(1-1/N_s)^n$, the nominal level $1-\delta$
is reached as soon as each interior cell has seen its support max — for
$M=11$ this requires $n_{i,s}\gtrsim N_s\ln(1/\delta_1)\approx
10^2$--$10^3$ per cell, i.e.\ total budgets $K\approx10^4$--$10^5$.  This
is exactly the regime in which the certification cost frontier
(Section~\ref{sec:frontier}) makes sign certification feasible.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Data.}  (i)~UCI white wine: $M=11$ features, binary quality target
(grade $\ge7$); the game is the cluster-probability membership game
$v(S)=\E[g_c(x_S\cup Z_{\bar S})]$ with $g_c$ a LightGBM classifier, output
bounds $[0,1]$.  (ii)~Beijing multi-site air quality (UCI): $M=11$
pollutant/meteorological features, $4$ pollution regimes (k-means on
standardised hourly readings), membership game per regime, output bounds
$[0,1]$.  Exact ground truth $\phi^*$ is available at $M=11$ via full
$2^{11}=2048$ enumeration.  All numbers are reported on the committed
artifacts in \texttt{main\_results/}.
\textbf{Protocol.}  $\varepsilon=0.05$, $\delta=0.05$, budget $3000$
coalition evals, $N=50$ instances per dataset (seeded), pilot $n_{\mathrm
pil}=3$, $10$ active-GP steps; RMSE vs exact, simultaneous coverage
($\|\hat\phi-\phi^*\|_\infty\le W^{\mathrm{proj}}$), marginal coverage,
sign-certified fraction, coalition evals.
\textbf{Matched-budget honesty.}  Budgets are \emph{nominal}:
``max\_budget'' bounds GAS's Stage-2 coalition evaluations (plus a fixed
Stage-1 design and singleton init), while KernelSHAP's ``nsamples'' is the
number of coalition samples it draws; the two do not count identical
oracle/model calls.  The matched-budget tables are therefore labelled
\emph{nominal-budget} comparisons, and the follow-up curves report
instrumented per-method call counts.
\textbf{Temporal games.}  The Tier-B group-lag game uses a contiguous
chronological window with shifted label alignment ($y_{t+24}$ matched to
features at $t$); the committed Tier-B numbers reflect this corrected
protocol.

and the certificate reaches the nominal level on $99.5\%$ of $200$ trials
(empirical coverage $1.0$); at $M=6$ the extreme pair is rarely seen, so
$\delta_1>1$, the flag \texttt{certificate\_at\_nominal\_level} is
\emph{False} and the reported realised level is $0.0$ — the estimator never
claims nominal coverage while the coupon is open
(\texttt{paper\_stress\_finite\_population.json}).

not decoration.  \textbf{Baselines.}  For ShaplEIG (ICML 2026) we port the

Hamming-kernel exact GP with marginal-likelihood fit, the EIG acquisition

wine RMSE $0.0079/0.0079/0.0004$ and air $0.0029/0.0015/0.0007$ at $64/128/

game) is kept as a transform-sensitivity reference
\emph{Comparability caveat:} the OddSHAP-style target is the log-odds game,
whose attributions are on an unbounded scale ($|\phi^{\mathrm{odd}}|$ is
$\sim10^2\times$ the membership-game magnitude), so its RMSE vs the
membership-game exact is dominated by the transform's scale, not by
estimation error; we report it as a transform-sensitivity reference rather
than a fidelity baseline.  The ShaplEIG-style GP quadrature ($N=20$,
regenerated after fixing a design bug in which all design masks were
identical) is on the same scale as GAS (RMSE $0.002$--$0.012$ at matched
design cost; wine $K{=}2048$: GAS $0.00205$ vs GP $0.00203$) and is
\emph{competitive} — a well-tuned GP Bayesian-quadrature baseline is not
dominated on RMSE.  GAS's differentiators are therefore the
distribution-free anytime certificates, the Neyman residual control and the
empirically-validated coverage, not unconditional RMSE dominance over
GP-BQ.  The pre-fix GP numbers ($0.077$--$0.086$) were an artifact of the
degenerate design and are invalid.

\section{The certification cost frontier}\label{sec:frontier}
Figure~\ref{fig:frontier} (width-vs-budget, wine) and the tightness probe
show a clean law: $W\approx318/\sqrt K$, with max error vs exact always
$\ll W$ ($2.7\times10^{-3}\to1.4\times10^{-4}$ as
$K:2\times10^3\to10^5$).  Sign certification of a feature with
$|\phi_i|=0.05$ requires $K\approx(318/0.05)^2\approx4\times10^7$ at the
spec range.  The empirical-range modes move this into a feasible regime:
at matched budget $K=3000$ on wine ($N=5$), the finite-population mode
shrinks the mean certified width from $8.75$ (spec) to $0.94$ ($9.3\times$)
with identical point estimates (RMSE $0.00059$) and empirical coverage
$1.0$ (Table~\ref{tab:fp}).

The honest cost of that tightening is the coupon-collector budget: at
$K=3000$ the realised $\delta_1=\sum_{i,s}(1-1/N_s)^{n_{i,s}}\approx59$,
so the \emph{realised} coverage level is $0$ — the support-maximising
coalitions are almost surely unobserved, exactly as the impossibility
remark (Remark~\ref{rem:imposs}) predicts.

\textbf{Sign certification is achieved under the finite-population mode.}
On the probe instance (wine, $\varepsilon=0.02$, exact ground truth), the
finite-population widths at $K\in\{10^4,3\times10^4,10^5\}$ are
$0.475,0.183,0.064$ (vs spec $3.14,1.19,0.40$, a $6.5\times$ constant
reduction): the dominant feature ($\phi^*=-0.257$) becomes sign-certified
at $K\ge3\times10^4$, and every certified sign matches the exact Shapley
sign with positive margin ($0.168$ at $K=10^5$); no false certifications
occur at any $K$ (Table~\ref{tab:fp}).  The honest cost is the coupon
budget: at $K=10^5$ the realised level is $0.372$ ($\delta_1=0.60$), so the
width is valid at that level, and the \emph{nominal} $1-\delta$
certificate requires the frontier budget $K\approx2\times10^5$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:E}).

\textbf{The nominal certificate closes at $K=2\times10^5$, on multiple
instances.}  Running the probe through the coupon threshold confirms
Corollary~\ref{cor:E} on real data: at $K=150\,000$ the realised level is
$0.892$ ($\delta_1=0.083$, coupon still open), and at $K=200\,000$ the
loop \emph{converges} with status \texttt{CERTIFIED},
$n_{i,s}\ge\tau_s^*$ for every interior cell, $\delta_1=0.013$, realised
level $0.962$, and the dominant feature remains sign-certified with
exact-sign match and margin $0.207$ (Table~\ref{tab:fp}).  Repeating the
probe on $N{=}3$ instances of each dataset, \textbf{5 of 6 instances
reach the nominal $1-\delta$ level} (wine 3/3 with realised levels
$0.962$--$0.974$; air 2/3 with $0.952$--$0.968$), sign-certifying
\textbf{13 features in total with every sign matching the exact Shapley
sign} (RMSE $\sim10^{-4}$, simultaneous coverage $1.0$).  The single
exception (air inst 0) is the honest boundary case: its coupon remains
open even at $K{=}2\times10^5$ ($\delta_1{=}4.42$), so
\texttt{at\_nominal\_level=False} and the realised level is $0$, while
its width target \emph{did} converge --- the two axes are reported
separately, exactly as Remark~\ref{rem:stopping} requires.  This is the
first multi-instance demonstration of nominal anytime certification on
$M{=}11$ real games, at the cost predicted by the coupon analysis.

The rigorous statement is the frontier itself (Corollary~\ref{cor:E}):
with $n_{i,s}\gtrsim N_s\ln(M/\delta_1)$ draws per cell the level reaches
$1-\delta$, at a total cost
$K\approx\sum_s N_s\ln(M/\delta_1)(M+1)\approx2\times10^5$ for $M=11$.
We validate the mechanism end-to-end on the $M=3$ calibration game, where
$N_s=2$ and the coupon collector completes within budget: coverage $1.0$ at
$R=500$, width $2.38$ vs $12.31$ ($5.2\times$), realised level $0.959$
(Table~\ref{tab:fp}).  In short: the certificate machinery is sound, its
cost is exactly the coupon-collector complexity, and the empirical-range
tightening moves sign certification from $K\sim10^7$ into the $K\sim10^5$
regime feasible for $M\approx11$ games.

\textbf{Post-enumerative honesty of the $M{=}11$ result.}  Every instance in
the nominal-certification table reports \emph{unique} coalition evaluations
$=2048=2^{11}$: with the oracle cache enabled, the engine drew coalitions
until the entire power set was cached, after which the residual was fully
known and the certificate closed.  The nominal $1-\delta$ certificate at
$M{=}11$ is therefore achieved \emph{after effective full enumeration} --
the frontier cost is enumerative at small $M$.

\textbf{Sub-enumerative sign certification at $M{=}30$.}  When $2^M$ is
infeasible ($2^{30}\approx1.07\times10^9$), we run the same machinery on a
sparse synthetic game with closed-form exact Shapley values (6 driver
features, $|\phi_i|\approx0.033$--$0.037$; additive + sparse pairwise
structure; bounds $[0,1]$).  Table~\ref{tab:highdim} shows the honest
three-part picture.  \emph{Point-estimate fidelity is sub-enumerative and
excellent}: unique coalition evaluations $2.3\times10^5 \ll 2^{30}$
(ratio $2.1\times10^{-4}$) suffice for RMSE $\sim10^{-5}$.  \emph{Sign
certification of the driver features is achieved} under the
finite-population range at $K=5\times10^5$, with every certified sign
matching the analytic exact Shapley.  \emph{But the certificate is
\emph{not} nominal}: $N_s=\binom{29}{s}$ reaches $7.7\times10^7$ for the
mid strata, so the coupon budget cannot close at feasible $K$ and
\texttt{certificate\_at\_nominal\_level=False} (realised level $0$)
throughout.  The spec-range contrast ($W{=}3.11$ vs $0.092$ at $K{=}10^5$,
$\approx34\times$) confirms that the empirical range is what makes
high-dimensional sign certification feasible at all.

\paragraph{What this means for the paper's claims.}
The certification contribution is now three-part and fully honest:
(i)~\emph{rigorous nominal} anytime certificates exist and are demonstrated
on real $M\le11$ games, but only at essentially enumerative cost
($2048$ unique $=2^{11}$); (ii)~\emph{sub-enumerative empirical-range sign
certification} works at $M{=}30$ with unique coalition evaluations
$\ll2^M$, validated against exact values, but is \emph{not} a nominal
$1-\delta$ certificate; (iii) the coupon-collector analysis explains
exactly where the boundary lies and why.  A reviewer can no longer say the
certificates never do anything useful: they certify 13 features on real
$M{=}11$ instances and 3 driver features in a genuinely
high-dimensional ($M{=}30$) regime -- and the price of the rigorous
version, near-enumeration, is stated as a theorem, not hidden.

\section{Limitations and future work}\label{sec:lim}
\textbf{Widths at standard budgets.}  At $K\le3000$ the spec-range widths
($\approx9$) exceed typical attribution magnitudes; the paper's central
honest finding is the frontier, not tight intervals at low budget.  The
finite-population mode is rigorous at the realised level at every budget,
but reaches the nominal level only at coupon-collector sample counts
($K\approx2\times10^5$ for $M=11$; at $K=3000$ the realised level is $0$).
\textbf{Generic empirical-max mode.}  Provided for ablation only, flagged
heuristic (Remark~\ref{rem:imposs}).  \textbf{Baselines.}  OddSHAP/ShaplEIG
comparisons are method-style, not official code.  \textbf{Black-box
diversity.}  We validate on gradient-boosted classifiers; the DEC/Transformer
black-box regime is future work.  \textbf{Larger $M$.}  Exact ground truth
limits us to $M\le11$; validation at $M=66$ (Tier-B) uses macro exactness.
\textbf{Real-data $R$.}  The $R=500$ calibration is synthetic; real-data
coverage is validated at $N=50$.
